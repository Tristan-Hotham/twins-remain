## Summary of the contribution (for a cover letter)

Town twinning was one of the most widespread forms of organised European exchange in British localities, and transactionalist theory expects cross-border contact to build attachment to Europe. This paper builds an open national inventory of British twinnings from Wikidata (1,445 links, 79% with EU partners), assigns them to the 632 constituencies of Great Britain with documented rules, and tests in an analysis pre-specified in a written plan whether seats with an EU twin had a lower estimated Leave share in 2016. Conditional on region, the standard census predictors and settlement size and documentation, the difference is -0.3 points (95% CI -1.4 to 0.8), null in all but two of 15 planned checks (an equivalence test at one point is not passed). A built-in placebo, twinning outside the EU, is associated with a slightly lower Leave share that disappears within local authorities, which points to selection into twinning rather than European contact. The paper contributes a reproducible dataset, a place-level null that excludes large associations but not one of about a point, and a design that separates European contact from the kind of place that twins. It is correlational and reports the uneven coverage of its source.
